Photonic integrated circuits: an introduction

Where the field came from, what gets integrated, and the one design move that turns a table of optics into a circuit.

[ 01 ]|what integrated photonics is

Several photonic components, one common planar substrate

In the broad sense, integrated photonics is the fabrication and integration of several photonic components on a common planar substrate.

That last phrase is the whole difference from regular optics. We are talking about circuits on a planar substrate, not elements arranged on a table.

Each component keeps its own functionality. The platform is the integration; the components are the vocabulary.

[ 02 ]|the component vocabulary

What can be put on the substrate

splitter / combiner
One beam in, two out. Reciprocal: run it backwards to combine.
grating
Filters light by wavelength; the diffraction workhorse.
coupler
Moves light between two components or two guides.
polariser
Selects or rotates one polarisation out of a scrambled input.
interferometer
Split, propagate, recombine. Already a small circuit.
light source
Generates photons on chip.
detector
Generated photons have to be counted somewhere.
and more
Modulators, amplifiers, phase shifters, resonators.

Each block has one functionality. Integrated photonics is the platform that lets several of them share one substrate.

[ 03 ]|a discrete element

Beam splitting is reciprocal

A beam splitter takes a single beam and produces two. Power splitting in one direction.

Run the same device the other way and it is a combiner. Splitting and combining are one reciprocal functionality.

Splitter, grating, coupler, polariser: each of these is a discrete element. None of them is yet a circuit.

[ 04 ]|the first circuit

An interferometer is already a circuit

Divide the light in two, let each half propagate along its own path, then put them back together. The two beams interfere.

Change the relative phase between the arms and the output swings between bright and dark. Drag to try it.

[ 05 ]|closing the loop

Generate photons, then count them

A light source on chip generates photons. Once photons exist, they have to be detected, so a detector is the matching on-chip component.

Source, waveguide, functional blocks, detector. That chain is what makes the substrate a platform and not a parts bin.

[ 06 ]|historic perspective

Optics: generation, propagation, interaction

Optics is the branch of physical science that deals with the generation and propagation of light and its interaction with matter. Light science, in short.

  • Lenses collimate a diverging beam: control of propagation.
  • In an isotropic medium light sees no direction; in an anisotropic crystal the propagation depends on direction. Linear and nonlinear crystals are the interaction half.

[ 07 ]|historic perspective

Three inventions made modern optics

  1. oneThe laser. Highly spatially and temporally coherent light with very high brightness. Coherence plus brightness opened optical signal processing and a new way to study materials.
  2. twoSemiconductor optical devices. Microelectronics and compound-semiconductor materials science met optics. Direct band gap materials generate and detect photons efficiently; the term optoelectronic arrives.
  3. threeCheap, low-loss optical fiber. Not just the invention but the fabrication: transport light from A to B without losing it. Loss today sits near the theoretical, material-limited floor.

[ 08 ]|semiconductor optical devices

Direct versus indirect band gap

materialemit photonsdetect photonsrole in photonics
direct gapefficientefficientlasers, LEDs, detectors
indirect gapinefficientusabledetectors, not sources

Compound semiconductors let the composition tune the band gap. Engineering the material generates new wavelengths that were simply unavailable before. Rich for materials science, useful for applications: efficient and compact optoelectronic devices.

[ 09 ]|what pushed optics toward photonics

From studying light to manipulating it

Photonics grew out of its narrow definition (photons interacting with electrons) into the umbrella term for general optical functionality. Nobody says optics much any more; everything is photonics.

[ 11 ]|electro-optics

An electrical signal, printed onto light

Take a continuous wave and change its intensity electrically.

  • Direct modulation: change the current driving the laser.
  • Electro-absorption: the material's absorption follows the applied field, so the output intensity follows the electrical pulse.

[ 12 ]|acousto-optics

Sound waves as a tunable grating

If an electric field can steer light, so can an acoustic wave. A piezo-electric transducer launches a pressure wave into the crystal; its periodic density is a grating that light diffracts from.

The acoustic wave can also come from phonons in the material rather than a transducer. Different origin, same interaction: acoustic wave meets light wave.

[ 13 ]|opto-electronics and quantum optics

Electrons in, photons out. And then single photons.

opto-electronics The process runs in the electronic domain but ends in photon generation, or begins because a photon was detected. Semiconductor LEDs, lasers, and detectors.
quantum optics Stop treating light as a photon flux. Generate a single photon, set its polarisation, interfere two photons of different states, detect it. Applications: secure key distribution, optical computing, imaging, sensing.

Optical amplifiers are quantum devices. Strictly, every photonic process is explained by quantum mechanics; quantum optics is where that stops being a footnote.

[ 14 ]|application disciplines

Generate, transport, manipulate: the applications follow

Short-distance and long-distance data communication were enabled by photonics and are studied as part of it. The rest of the list exists because we learned to generate, transport, and manipulate light well.

[ 15 ]|integrated photonics, historically

The term is older than you think

  1. 1960sThe proposal. Emphasise the similarity between planar circuit technology and the well established integrated microelectronic circuit. Optical benches look like circuits; why not build them like one?
  2. post-1980sIts own structure. The field stops mimicking microelectronics and develops a shape of its own, but the core concept was already established: many components on a single substrate.
  3. todayGuided waves plus all associated disciplines. Electro-optics, acousto-optics, nonlinear optics, and opto-electronics, together on one substrate.

[ 16 ]|the one design move

On the table, light travels in free space

On an optical table there is no guiding mechanism. The sources are coherent, the beams barely diverge, and you can manage the alignment by hand. No waveguides anywhere.

[ 16 ]|the one design move

On the chip, light has to be guided

Put the circuit on a substrate and every component must be connected. Light has to be guided from one block to the next. The waveguide is the backbone of integrated photonics.

Guided-wave technology is what the 1960s proposal added to the microelectronics analogy.

[ 17 ]|putting it together

Integrated photonics, defined

Combine guided-wave technology with all of the associated disciplines, on a single substrate.

Note what the definition does not say: nothing about a particular material system. It is a philosophy of bringing components and functionalities onto one substrate through a guided-wave mechanism.

what to remember Integrated photonics is components with individual functionalities, connected by guided waves, on one planar substrate. The laser, the semiconductor optical device, and low-loss fiber made photonics; the 1960s microelectronics analogy made it a circuit.
